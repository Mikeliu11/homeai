# DESIGN

**One idea.** The model reads; the engine prices; Jordan decides. Claude turns messy texts and screenshots into *proposals* (structured terms, bill line items). A pure Go pricing engine reprices every bill line against the confirmed terms in force on that date and produces three numbers: what the helper claimed, what the lines add to, and what the agreement supports. Nothing becomes a record until Jordan clicks Confirm; nothing is payable until Jordan clicks Approve; approved bills are immutable.

**What the assistant remembers vs. what the system records.** AgentCore Memory holds the conversation (short-term events per session, replayed for continuity) and household context extracted by the semantic strategy or written by an explicit `remember` tool: who Sam is, that Lupe has a key, how each helper is paid. DynamoDB holds commitments: helpers, confirmed terms snapshots, bills, approvals, audit events. The engine never reads Memory, and the prompt labels memories "unverified recollections, never agreed terms". If a memory and a record disagree, the record wins and the assistant says so. Money facts mentioned in chat must become a terms proposal, not a memory. Why: memory is probabilistic and extracted asynchronously; money needs a thing Jordan confirmed, with a source and a date.

**Where the confirmations sit.** The agent's tools can read records and create proposals, turns and memories; proposals are validated (rule kinds, clocks, dates, amounts) and refused with a message the model can act on, and a new proposal for the same helper and period (or effective date) supersedes the earlier open one, so a self-correction never leaves two live cards. It has no IAM permission to write the Records table; Confirm, Approve, Hold and Reject are REST endpoints bound to UI buttons, served by a separate Lambda. Confirm and Approve carry a content hash of what the UI rendered; a mismatch is refused. Approval recomputes the check first, so a terms change confirmed after the bill was checked is caught. One deliberate exception: Settings → *Start over* wipes the whole household (records, uploads, memory) behind a typed RESET and leaves a single audit entry; approved bills are immutable to edits, not to starting over, and the reset and conversation delete (Settings → History) are the only routes that delete anything. Every flagged line (unagreed, ambiguous, unpriceable) must be explicitly included or excluded in the approve dialog; an unpriceable line forces a typed amount and a note. The content hash is recomputed from the stored payload at confirm time, so a tampered proposal row cannot be confirmed either. A typed amount that differs from what the engine computed needs a note. Prompt injection inside a screenshot or paste is covered by the same boundary: the model cannot commit anything, whatever it reads.

**When inputs disagree.** Terms are full snapshots keyed by `effectiveFrom`; the one in force for a date is the latest `effectiveFrom` ≤ date, so the order Jordan pastes things does not matter (the Lupe September change can be confirmed before the January thread). A later dated message beats an earlier one; any dated message beats Jordan's undated note, which becomes a snapshot with no date so earlier months can still be checked. The card lists conflicts and assumptions ("starting the 15th" in an Aug 28 text = Sep 15, inclusive). Third-party say-so ("Sam said that was fine") is extracted as an unagreed line with the claim quoted, never as agreement. When the agreed rates admit two readings of the same line (Sunday after 10pm: late rate × Sunday multiplier, or whichever is higher; two multipliers on one day: stacked or the larger alone; a terms change inside the period on undated lines) the engine returns both and marks the line ambiguous rather than choosing; Jordan picks a reading in the approve dialog. Two base hourly rates for one service, or a per-mile rule with no miles, are unpriceable, never a silent pick. A more expensive alternative reading (Sat 9/12 as a date night, $128 vs $122 billed) is shown as information, not a problem.

**Deliberate engine choices.** Integer cents, exact minutes, no rounding unless the agreement has a rounding term. "After 10pm" with no end stays on through midnight within a booking. A flat package covers one evening on the booking's date and the overflow is priced as one piece, so a date night that runs to 1am gets the late rate through midnight. A flat package is an alternative reading only when the booking runs to the package's end time (a Sunday-morning sit is not a date night). Date-only multipliers (Sundays, holidays) apply to per-visit and flat lines too, so "holidays double" works for the dog walker. A line billed under the agreed amount is paid as billed, flagged for information, never topped up without a decision. Undated aggregate lines (Theo's "18 × $28") take the period; if the rate changed inside the period they are ambiguous with both prices. Bill-level flags: arithmetic, duplicate lines, dates outside the period, holidays, non-usual days, count vs. usual days, dates already on another bill. One open bill per helper and period: re-pasting replaces the open bill (the card says so) rather than creating a second payable record, and a month that is already approved refuses a new bill.

**Known failure modes.** Mis-extraction: a misread "$130" becomes a wrong line; mitigated by showing the source beside the extracted lines and by the hash on confirm, never by trusting the model. Cropped or missing dates: the agent proposes with an assumption and asks. Memory lag (~1 minute) and read-after-write delays on explicit memories. Bedrock throttling on new accounts: retried with backoff, then surfaced on the turn as a readable error rather than a hung "thinking". A Lambda crash or timeout: the turn is claimed once, the agent runs under a deadline 20 s short of Lambda's, and the API reports any turn silent for 5.5 minutes as failed. Chat is asynchronous (POST a turn, poll it) so a slow multi-image turn never hits API Gateway's 30 s limit. The flat-package heuristic can miss an unusual package shape; when it does the line still prices under the hourly rules and the alternative shows as information. A "4 hours, $88" line with no times is priced at the base rate with a note that evening or Sunday rates could not be checked.

**Left out, on purpose.** Real payments and bank links. Notifications, email/SMS ingestion. Multiple households or users. OCR service (Claude reads images directly). AgentCore Runtime (the brief leaves hosting to us; a Lambda is one fewer readiness state to fail on a first deploy), Gateway and Identity. The user-preference memory strategy. A full rule editor (amounts and dates are editable on a card; anything else is "reject and tell the assistant"). Post-approval adjustments. Timezones beyond one household constant. Streaming responses.
